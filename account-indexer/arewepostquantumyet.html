What does post-quantum mean?

To verify that you own an account and can send transactions from it, you need to own a key, that only you and nobody else knows. In order to verify ownership of that key without showing it to anyone, a lot of complex math is used. Most of blockchains and accounts use math of elliptic curves, which has been really reliable and secure over the last decades, but in the next years will be at risk of being broken by quantum computers.

So very smart people made even more complex math, that even quantum computers cannot break, which is called "post-quantum cryptography", and NEAR is the first big blockchain to add support for that math natively, and teams at Meteor Wallet and Intear Wallet already support using it for everyday transactions. Thanks to NEAR's access model, you can safely switch your existing accounts to quantum-safe keys.

Mainnet

How do I switch my account to quantum-safe key?

Open Intear Wallet, navigate to Settings and click on "Switch to quantum-safe key". This is a quick and safe process that will take 2 seconds, will let you keep your account ID, all assets on it, and same seed phrase (phrase will only work in Intear Wallet because seed phrase for post-quantum is not yet standardized). You can switch back at any time by following the same process.

Advanced users only

These steps are for people who are comfortable managing access keys by hand. Although it's probably impossible to lock yourself out of your account, take caution or try other methods if you're not comfortable with key management.

Open Meteor Wallet, navigate to Settings, click on "Security and Recovery", then "Manage Full Access Keys", click on "Add New Access Key", enter your password, click "Generate New Key", then choose "ML-DSA-65", save the long key that starts with "ml-dsa-65:", and click on "Add ml-dsa-65 key" to proceed. Then in the same "Manage Full Access Keys" menu, find a key that starts with "ml-dsa-65-hash:" and has "Set as Primary Key" button. Click that button to make the wallet use the new key for all transactions, and enter password again. After that, this key will be labeled as "Meteor Primary Key". Now you need to get rid of the old, insecure keys that still exist and can compromise your account: click on "Revoke Access" for every single key you find there, except for the primary one (it will not have that button). Click "Remove Key" to confirm, and enter your password again. Once you're done, enter your account ID above to check that your account is using post-quantum cryptography and is safe from quantum attacks. Your seed phrase will stop working, and you will not get a new seed phrase, you will have to use the long key you saved earlier to sign in to your account.

If you're a developer, you can use near-cli to manually rotate your account to quantum-safe keys. First, add a new key to your account: 

near account add-key <ACCOUNT_ID> grant-full-access autogenerate-new-keypair --signature-scheme ml-dsa-65 save-to-keychain network-config mainnet sign-with-keychain

Then delete the old key:

near account delete-keys <ACCOUNT_ID>

In the interactive mode, select the non-quantum-safe keys to delete (deleting post-quantum keys is not possible), and confirm the transaction.

HOT Wallet does not support post-quantum cryptography yet, but you still have at least a few years before quantum computers will be able to break it, so you can wait until support is added.

MyNearWallet is officially deprecated by its developers. Its maintainers recommend migrating to Meteor Wallet, and they've already added support for post-quantum cryptography to it, so that's the recommended alternative, you'll have to migrate soon anyway.

Or if your primary reason to stay on MyNearWallet is it being open-source, Intear Wallet is open-source and has post-quantum support. It's actually even more open, since you can self-host the wallet and connect a self-hosted wallet to dApps.

Just owning a hardware wallet makes you safer against many attacks, but not against quantum computers. Hardware wallets at the moment have no support for post-quantum cryptography on NEAR. You can either migrate an existing account from Ledger to Intear Wallet (has safe key migration while keeping the same account ID), or keep using your hardware wallet until absolutely unavoidable: you probably still have a few years of safety in pre-quantum era, and developers will likely add support for post-quantum cryptography in the future.

Validators

safe pre-quantum
34% 67%

 

Validators are the second-most security-critical piece, right after your own wallet. If 67% of validators are hacked, they can take over the blockchain, and 34% can bring the blockchain to a temporary halt, that's why it's important that validators are using quantum-safe keys.

At the moment, pool.near validators don't have the ability to use post-quantum keys, but this will be changed, as pool.near pools are upgradable.

poolv1.near pools are not upgradable, and that's something NEAR will have to solve as part of the Roadmap to Post-Quantum Security.

Not quantum-safe

Chain Signatures

Can't produce signatures for post-quantum blockchains, can't securely bring data from other chains. Research is ongoing into post-quantum thresholded signatures, and other blockchains must go post-quantum too, since mutual post-quantum trust can't exist with a blockchain that is prone to quantum attacks. Read NEAR's Roadmap to Post-Quantum Security for more details.
